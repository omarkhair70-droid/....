## Meaning and algorithm
A goal is an explicitly authored outcome for a work revision, with contextual capability slots, a place identifier, a time window, minimum common duration, declared conditions and an explicit distinct-participants policy. An offer is a versioned *proposed* contribution by an identified participant: one exact capability, place, interval and required capability/availability proposition IDs. An offer is not a promise, verified availability, or consent. Goal and offer identities and revisions must be assigned by an upstream trusted boundary; this module does not resolve identities, prove sources, extract claims, or discover offers online.

Index scoped offers by capability after matching exact place IDs. Traverse slots and offers in stable ID order using bounded DFS, intersecting half-open windows incrementally and pruning branches whose common duration is insufficient. Each offer is used at most once; distinct participant policy is supplied by the goal, not inferred. No geographic or semantic fuzzy matching, travel, resource capacities or scheduling trade-offs yet. The common window is a proposed overlap, not a schedule. Preconditions from the goal are unioned with the capability and availability claims of each chosen contribution, with hard taking precedence. At most 8 slots, 256 offers, 16 base conditions, 10k explored extensions and 64 results; output marks limit exhaustion rather than implying exhaustive search. No ranking score or claim of optimality. Search order is deterministic but not a relevance ranking. Empty completed result means only no match within the provided structured offers, not no business possibility in reality.

The structural key binds scope, work and goal revisions plus exact slot/offer/revision/participant assignments. It is not a hash, global identity or permission. Upstream must ensure immutable inputs at a particular revision and persist the mapping to a canonical candidate ID; same-version conflicting input across separate calls is not detectable here. Proposal includes window/place and can explicitly materialize a Candidate with supplied ID; evaluation is through the existing engine registration. The candidate evaluator currently stores the roles/requirements and work revision, not the full structural key or window; persist and revalidate both before acting. No automatic acceptance, state writes, or external communication. No action based solely on supported evidence. Scope checks require an already-authorized snapshot; caller must enforce scope, source and grant lifecycle. Future time windows are hypotheses: evidence is evaluated at explicit engine time, not a guarantee for a future date.
